# PhET-Style Think-Aloud Protocol (10 Minutes, Silent Observer)

Use this 1-page script when watching Kym test a new or revised figure in **The Patterned World**, **Atlas**, or **Euclid's Workshop**. The goal is to separate **control friction** (where she is confused about what to touch) from **mathematical surprise** (where the model teaches her something she did not expect).

---

## Before You Start

1. Open the chapter or shape lab cleanly to **Look first** (`openPlay`), with controls reset and sound muted or low.
2. Tell Kym: *"We are testing whether this picture explains itself well, not testing you. Think out loud whenever you try something—say what you expect to happen before you drag or tap."*
3. Keep a pen and scratch paper next to the iPad/Mac. Do **not** steer her mouse or point at controls.

---

## The 10-Minute Script

| Minute | Phase | What Kym Does | Observer Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0:00 – 3:00** | **Open Play** | Explores the figure freely before any question or prompt. | **Stay completely silent.** Notice which controls she touches first and which controls she ignores. |
| **3:00 – 7:00** | **Task & Prediction** | Moves through **Strip away → Symbols → Challenge** (or 2–3 Quadrilateral missions). Asks *"What will happen if I double this / drag corner B?"* before moving. | **Wait 10 full seconds** before answering any *"Wait, how do I…?"* question. Answer with *"What do you notice if you try touching it?"* first. |
| **7:00 – 10:00** | **Device-Off Debrief** | Closes or folds away the figure and answers the **Device off** check on paper (or pins two snapshots side by side and explains what stayed equal). | Ask one question only: *"In your own words, what stayed the same while everything else moved?"* |

---

## Tell Control Friction Apart from Maths Misconceptions

- **Control / UI Friction (fix the figure or label):**
  - She clicks a non-interactive label or diagram part expecting it to drag.
  - She misses a handle twice because the hit target is too tight, or scrolls the page instead of dragging.
  - She asks *"What does this word/toggle mean?"* or does not notice an overlay until it moves.
- **Mathematical Surprise / Misconception (valuable—keep or sharpen this beat):**
  - She expects a square tilted at the top to stop being a parallelogram, or predicts a median shifts when the largest dot moves right.
  - She toggles **Zero Pairs** or **Lock Ratio** and pauses because the result contradicts her prediction.
  - She sees a **cusp corner** jump on the derivative graph or discovers two unequal pans still balance when $x$ is negative.
